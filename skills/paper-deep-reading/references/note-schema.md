## TL;DR
Explain the problem, core idea, main result, and why it matters.

## Critical Take
Give a concise, fact-grounded judgment on the paper's real value, biggest weakness, possible overclaim, or most fragile assumption.

## Research Question
What exact question is the paper trying to answer?

## Motivation and Basic Idea
Explain why the problem matters, what current practice fails to handle, the simplest form of the idea, and why it is plausible.

## Background
Only include the minimum chain needed to understand:
background → problem → gap → proposed direction.

## Main Contributions
Separate methodological, theoretical, empirical, systems, or dataset contributions when applicable.

## Method
Explain how the core idea becomes the actual method.

FAST: focus on the central mechanism.
DEEP: include derivation structure, dependencies, and technical details when relevant.

## Threat Model / Assumptions

## Evaluation

### Setup

- baselines
- metrics

### Main Findings
FAST: only the main evidence.
DEEP: include ablations, sensitivity analyses, negative results, and reproduction details when relevant.

## Key Figures / Tables / Equations

DEEP: all central artifacts needed to reconstruct the evidence.

## Strengths
Concrete strengths only.

## Limitations
Concrete limitations only.

## My Takeaways
Record reusable ideas, theorem patterns, experimental designs, and connections to current projects.

## What Deserves Deeper Reading
FAST: list sections/theorems/figures worth revisiting.
DEEP: list unresolved technical questions or points worth checking again.

## Related Papers
List only genuinely relevant papers and explain the relationship.

## Open Questions
List concrete, actionable research questions prompted by the paper.
